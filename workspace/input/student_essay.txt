Social media platforms such as Instagram and Threads have become deeply embedded in the daily routines of university students in Hong Kong. While these digital networks offer instant connectivity and peer support, growing empirical evidence suggests that excessive screen time significantly heightens anxiety and depressive symptoms among local youth (Smith, 2023). In Hong Kong's highly competitive academic environment, constant exposure to curated lifestyles intensifies upward social comparison and Fear of Missing Out (FOMO), which gradually erodes students' self-esteem. Furthermore, late-night scrolling disrupts circadian rhythms and reduces sleep quality, directly impairing daytime cognitive performance in lectures (Chan & Lee, 2024). However, banning social media entirely is neither realistic nor effective for digital natives. Instead, universities in Hong Kong should integrate digital literacy workshops and mindfulness-based interventions into general education curricula so students can cultivate healthier, more intentional online habits.
